# Comedy Structure Templates

## Core Principle
Specificity is comedy. The more precise the detail, the funnier the observation.
Truth is funnier than exaggeration—the real stories are absurd enough.

---

## Joke Structures

### 1. The Credential Undercut
Setup establishes claimed expertise, punchline reveals actual knowledge.

**Formula:** "[They claim X years/credentials]. [What that actually means]."

**Examples:**
- "He's got 15 years of CX experience—which means he's been wrong about chatbots three separate times."
- "She's a 'digital transformation expert'—she's transformed three companies' budgets into consulting fees."
- "Certified AI strategist. The certification took 4 hours and cost $299."

---

### 2. The Technical Truth Bomb
State the vendor/initiative promise, then the actual technical reality.

**Formula:** "They promised [X]. They delivered [precise, devastating Y]."

**Examples:**
- "They promised 95% accuracy. They delivered 95% confidence in wrong answers."
- "They promised real-time insights. The dashboard refreshes every 47 minutes."
- "They promised AI-powered automation. They delivered an if-then statement with a ChatGPT wrapper."

---

### 3. The Insider Callback
Reference something only people in the industry would recognize. The humor comes from shared pain.

**Formula:** "You know [situation is doomed] when [specific insider signal]."

**Examples:**
- "You know the implementation is in trouble when they start calling it a 'journey.'"
- "You know the vendor is lying when they say 'our other clients don't have this problem.'"
- "You know the project is dead when it gets moved to the 'Center of Excellence.'"
- "You know leadership has given up when they ask for 'quick wins.'"

---

### 4. The Escalating Specificity
Start broad, get painfully specific. Each beat more precise than the last.

**Formula:** "[Broad claim]. Specifically, [narrower]. More specifically, [devastatingly precise]."

**Examples:**
- "AI will transform customer service. Specifically, it will transform your budget into a vendor's Q4 bonus. More specifically, into their sales team's President's Club trip to Cabo."
- "The platform will increase agent productivity. Specifically, productivity in alt-tabbing between seven systems. More specifically, productivity in explaining to customers why the system is down again."

---

### 5. The Timeline Reality Check
Contrast promised timeline with actual timeline.

**Formula:** "Timeline: [promised]. Actual timeline: [reality]."

**Examples:**
- "Implementation timeline: 12 weeks. Actual timeline: 12 weeks to select the vendor, 18 months to go live, 3 years to admit it doesn't work."
- "ROI timeline: 6 months. Actual timeline: 6 months to realize we can't measure it, 12 months to redefine success, 18 months to declare victory anyway."

---

### 6. The Definition Corruption
Take corporate jargon and define what it actually means.

**Formula:** "'[Corporate term]' (n.): [Brutal actual definition]."

**Examples:**
- "'Rightsizing' (n.): Layoffs with a PR team."
- "'Synergy' (n.): We fired the people who knew how things worked."
- "'AI-powered' (adj.): Has an API call to OpenAI somewhere in the codebase."
- "'Enterprise-grade' (adj.): Costs 10x more, works 10x slower."

---

### 7. The Meeting Autopsy
Describe a meeting type and what it actually accomplishes.

**Formula:** "The [meeting type]: [what it's supposed to do]. What actually happens: [reality]."

**Examples:**
- "The steering committee: Supposed to remove blockers. What actually happens: 12 people take turns asking questions answered in the pre-read nobody opened."
- "The vendor demo: Supposed to evaluate solutions. What actually happens: Sales rep clicks through rehearsed scenarios while IT silently catalogs everything that won't work in our environment."

---

### 8. The Resume Translation
What they say on LinkedIn vs. what actually happened.

**Formula:** "LinkedIn: '[Impressive claim].' Reality: [What actually happened]."

**Examples:**
- "LinkedIn: 'Led enterprise-wide AI transformation.' Reality: Bought a Salesforce license and left before implementation."
- "LinkedIn: 'Drove $10M in operational savings.' Reality: Laid off the team that would have noticed the number is made up."
- "LinkedIn: 'Pioneered innovative customer experience strategies.' Reality: Changed the hold music."

---

### 9. The Vendor Bingo
List the phrases that signal you're being sold vaporware.

**Formula:** "If they say [X], they mean [Y]."

**Examples:**
- "If they say 'our roadmap includes that,' they mean it doesn't exist."
- "If they say 'our other clients love it,' they mean they have no reference customers."
- "If they say 'minimal configuration required,' they mean you'll need 3 consultants for 6 months."
- "If they say 'AI-native architecture,' they mean they added ChatGPT last quarter."

---

### 10. The Witness Statement
Frame as something you personally observed. Adds authority and specificity.

**Formula:** "I watched [specific thing happen]. [What it revealed]."

**Examples:**
- "I watched a $3M AI initiative die because the security review required documentation that doesn't exist for any software on earth."
- "I watched a vendor demo 'real-time analytics' that was actually a pre-recorded video with a fake cursor."
- "I watched a steering committee debate font choices for 45 minutes while the project burned."

---

## Roast Structures

### The Triple Escalation
Three beats, each worse than the last.

**Example:**
"Their AI strategy has three phases:
1. Announce AI initiative to boost stock price
2. Hire consultants to explain what AI is
3. Quietly shelve project and claim 'lessons learned'"

### The Compliment Execution
Sounds like praise, lands as devastation.

**Example:**
"You have to admire their consistency—they've promised the same 'game-changing' features for three consecutive annual conferences."

### The Specific Callback
Reference earlier material for compounding effect.

**Example:**
"Remember when I said the implementation was 6 months away? Good news—it's still 6 months away. It's been 6 months away for 2 years."

---

## Rules for Maximum Impact

1. **Real beats exaggerated**: The actual stories are absurd enough
2. **Specific beats general**: "47 minutes" is funnier than "forever"
3. **Witnessed beats imagined**: "I watched" > "They probably"
4. **Technical accuracy matters**: People in-the-know will fact-check
5. **Punch up, not down**: Target vendors, executives, consultants—not individual contributors
6. **Leave room for recognition**: Best jokes make people think "that's exactly what happened to us"
